Sinclair ZX80

An emulator of Sinclair's 1980 home computer, built on Z80 Bench. The ZX80 has no video chip: the Z80 itself runs through the display file, and a handful of logic chips turn its refresh cycles into a TV picture. This emulator models that trick cycle by cycle, so the picture comes from the CPU exactly as on the real machine.

Open the emulator

Features

  • The real ROM: load your own 4 KB ZX80 ROM image (it isn't included); your browser remembers it. It boots to the K cursor and runs 4K Integer BASIC
  • Video from the CPU: character bytes become NOPs, the font is fetched during refresh cycles, HALT and the interrupt end each line, and a virtual TV locks onto the sync pulses. The screen blanks while BASIC is busy, just as the real one does
  • Keyboard: type on your keyboard, or click the on-screen ZX80 keys, which show each key's BASIC keyword and SHIFT symbol from the ROM's own tables
  • Memory: 1K as supplied, or 16K with the RAM pack
  • Real time: 3.25 MHz, or full speed
  • Debugging: Z80 Bench's tools, including the disassembler, memory view, breakpoints and stepping, plus a panel showing the video logic: line counter, sync and the character latch
