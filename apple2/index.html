Apple II Plus

An emulator of the Apple II Plus with the RFI revision of the main board (820-0044), made with 6502 Bench. It follows Jim Sather's Understanding the Apple II (1983) and Apple's schematic of the board: the picture is generated cycle by cycle from the video scanner's own addresses, as the board does it, so mode switches in the middle of a frame and the "floating bus" behave as on a real Apple. It runs the real Applesoft and Autostart Monitor ROMs, and shows what the hardware is doing while it runs.

Features

  • The main board: 16K, 32K or 48K of RAM in rows C, D and E, the six ROMs D0–F8, the keyboard, the speaker, the soft switches at C050h–C05Fh, two paddles, three push buttons and the four annunciators
  • Timing: 65 cycles a line with the long cycle, 262 lines a frame (17,030 cycles), 1.02 MHz; or 312 lines at 50 Hz with the Eurapple jumpers
  • Video: TEXT (normal, inverse and flashing), LORES, HIRES, MIXED and both pages, from the scanner's addresses one cycle at a time. A colour monitor shows the colours the way the Apple makes them, from the dot pattern (with HIRES colour fringes and coloured text in MIXED); or choose black and white, green or amber
  • The floating bus: an address nothing answers reads the byte the video scanner is fetching, as on the real machine
  • The real ROMs: point the emulator at the folder holding your own ROM images (they aren't included); it recognises them by their contents and your browser remembers them. A row of sockets shows what is fitted, and clicking a socket fits a ROM of your own there
  • Keyboard: type on your keyboard, or click the on-screen II Plus keyboard with CTRL, SHIFT, REPT and CTRL-RESET; paste text to type BASIC programs and Monitor commands in
  • Cassette: play recordings of Apple tapes (any audio file) into the cassette input, make a tape from a binary file for the Monitor's R, and record SAVE or W as a WAV file
  • Cards: a 16K language card in slot 0, and a Disk II controller in slot 6 with two drives: .dsk, .do, .po and .nib images, writing, write protect, and saving the disk back as an image. Booting needs the card's boot PROM, which is your own file
  • Memory chips and faults: the 24 4116 RAMs, the six ROMs and the text ROM, with their pins live. Click a pin to disconnect it or hold it high or low; a RAM fault shows in the picture too, because the video scanner reads the same chips
  • Debugging: 6502 Bench's tools, including the pinout, bus timing, disassembler, breakpoints and stepping, plus a panel with the soft switches, the keyboard latch, the paddle timers, the video scanner's position and the cards' state, a memory bitmap tinted by the screen pages and Applesoft's use of memory, and a text listing with the BASIC program
