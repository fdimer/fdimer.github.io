ZX81 ULA Test Bench

A ZX81 whose ULA is the real logic, not a model of its behaviour. The Ferranti 2C210 was transcribed gate for gate from Ferranti's own logic diagram, and a single-clock version was made from it for an FPGA. Here the Z80 and the TV picture get everything from that ULA's pins. It is for anyone who wants to help check the ULA against real programs; for using a ZX81, the ZX80 and ZX81 emulator is the one to open.

Open the test bench

Features

  • Two ULAs: the synchronous design (one 13 MHz clock, the Verilog that would go into an FPGA), which runs at about the speed of a real ZX81, and the gate-level netlist of the 2C210 with a delay on every gate, which runs about a hundred times slower
  • Nothing modelled: the opcode the Z80 reads (so the NOPs that draw the picture), NMI, WAIT, the memory selects, the font address, sync and video all come from the ULA's pins
  • Your own ROM and programs: load an 8 KB ZX81 ROM image (not included; your browser remembers it) and .p program files; the page types LOAD "" for you
  • Picture check: twice a second the dots on the ULA's TV pin are read back as characters with the ROM's font and compared with the display file in memory
  • Frame meters: the lines and sync of the last frame, and how many frames changed their line count
  • Logic analyser: the ULA's pins and some inside signals for the last 16,000 T-states, with stepping by instruction or frame, and a copy in VCD format for viewers such as GTKWave
  • Report: a summary of the run to paste into an issue or an email
  • Keyboard: your own, or the on-screen keys marked as on a ZX81

Not modelled

The tape signal (LOAD puts the program straight into memory), a RAM pack's own address decoding (the 16K is one block on the ULA's RAM select), and the analogue side of the pins.

Sources

Ferranti logic diagram LD6679/ULA2C210/1 (12 January 1982) and the cell circuits ULA 2C158/2, from the Ferranti archive catalogued in 2026 by Happy Little Diodes. The netlist, the Verilog, the simulator and how each was checked are in the repository.
